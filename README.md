# The Cross Section of Expected Stock Returns in Taiwan 

## Abstract
This study investigates the cross-sectional determinants of expected stock returns in the Taiwan stock market, focusing particularly on market beta, firm size, and book-to-market (BM) equity ratio. Utilizing data from companies listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange (TWSE) from July 2005 to June 2020, the analysis employs the Fama-MacBeth regression methodology to assess the explanatory power of these factors. Empirical findings indicate that firm size robustly explains the variation in expected returns, exhibiting a statistically significant negative relationship. Contrary to predictions from the Sharpe-Lintner Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM), market beta does not show significant explanatory power and even demonstrates negative coefficients. The BM ratio shows a positive but weaker association, becoming statistically insignificant when size is simultaneously considered, suggesting that size may subsume the explanatory power of the BM ratio in Taiwan. Preliminary industry-level analysis, particularly contrasting semiconductor and food industries, suggests potential heterogeneity in factor behavior across industries. The study highlights the need for further investigation at the industry level and careful consideration of econometric methods and theoretical interpretations.
